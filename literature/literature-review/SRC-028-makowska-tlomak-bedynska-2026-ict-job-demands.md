## Extraction
| Aspect | Content |
|---|---|
| Design/context | Study 1 cross-sectional (N=165); Study 2 three-wave longitudinal sample of workers aged 45+ (N T1=1,115; T2=799; T3=512). |
| Constructs | Specific ICT job demands including workload, hassles, poor communication; digital transformation stress; ICT skills. |
| Findings | ICT workload, hassles, and poor communication were persistent predictors of digital-transformation stress; high ICT skills did not eliminate stress responses. |
| Relevance | Supports construct separation between ICT job demands and digital skill/capability mismatch. |

## Critical appraisal
Outcome is digital-transformation stress rather than burnout, and the older-worker sample limits direct transfer. Longitudinal evidence nevertheless strengthens the demand/skill-boundary argument.

## Statement relations
SUPPORTS the bounded definition of DJD and LIMITS any attempt to treat low digital skill as the same construct as digital job demand.

## Synthesis patterns

| LIT | Pattern | Supporting sources | Counterexamples / qualifiers |
|---|---|---|---|
| LIT-001 | Specific digital work demands/stressors are repeatedly associated with exhaustion/burnout-related strain, but direct evidence using a distinct DJD construct in policing remains sparse. | SRC-012; SRC-013; SRC-020; SRC-021; SRC-022; SRC-028 | SRC-024 shows digital transition can be manageable/resource-supported; SRC-013 measures technology difficulty rather than DJD; SRC-022 exhaustion is not full burnout. |
| LIT-002 | POS/organizational support has comparatively consistent evidence as a lower-burnout/lower-exhaustion resource in policing, but buffering is not universal and depends on support type/path. | SRC-014; SRC-018; SRC-019; SRC-012 | SRC-021 found no focal buffering by workplace social support; support constructs are not interchangeable. |
| LIT-003 | Earlier focused synthesis: direct Burnout→POR evidence was not established and reverse/common-cause pathways were plausible. | SRC-023; SRC-027; SRC-016; SRC-010 | **SUPERSEDED by LIT-006 after Issue #32 directionality audit.** |
| LIT-004 | Employee-rated organizational resilience/POR is empirically possible but construct definitions and measurement levels vary; true OR in police/public-safety research is dominated by organizational capabilities such as leadership, communication, governance, resources, and learning. | SRC-010; SRC-016; SRC-024 | POR can overlap with readiness/agility/effectiveness perceptions; no universal instrument emerged from RUN-011. |
| LIT-005 | Digitalization has a dual empirical position: employee-level digital demands can generate strain, while organization-level digital capabilities can strengthen adaptation and true organizational resilience. | SRC-020; SRC-022; SRC-025; SRC-026; SRC-024 | The two pathways operate at different levels and should not be collapsed into a single positive or negative effect. |
| LIT-006 | Targeted directionality evidence is asymmetric: organizational/collective adaptive or resilience conditions more often appear upstream of employee health than burnout appears upstream of employee-rated organizational resilience. | SRC-023; SRC-027; SRC-029; SRC-030; SRC-031; SRC-032; SRC-033 | Some evidence is adjacent rather than exact POR; Scopus/WoS parity remains incomplete. |
| LIT-007 | Burnout/exhaustion can matter for performance-, OCB-, or safety-relevant outcomes, but mixed/null findings and construct mismatch do not establish Burnout→POR or causal bottom-up organizational resilience. | SRC-030; SRC-034; SRC-036 | SRC-035 reports a null burnout–performance relation; most evidence is cross-sectional and non-POR. |

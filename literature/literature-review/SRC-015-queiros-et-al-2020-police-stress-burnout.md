# SRC-015 — Queirós et al. (2020), police stress and burnout measurement

## Identity and provenance
- SRC ID: SRC-015
- Track: targeted empirical evidence for Issue #26
- Source type: peer-reviewed open-access journal article
- Metadata: Queirós, C., Passos, F., Bártolo, A., Marques, A. J., da Silva, C. F., & Pereira, A. (2020). Burnout and stress measurement in police officers: Literature review and a study with the Operational Police Stress Questionnaire. *Frontiers in Psychology, 11*, 587.
- DOI: 10.3389/fpsyg.2020.00587
- Empirical sample: 2,057 Portuguese National Police officers; literature-review component covered 108 studies
- Retrieval: publisher/PMC full text and Elicit, 2026-10-11
- Access: FULL_TEXT

## Extraction
The article shows that operational stress, distress, and burnout are distinct but related occupational-health domains in policing and emphasizes that measurement must reflect policing-specific work characteristics. Its empirical police sample demonstrates substantial heterogeneity in stress/burnout indicators depending on construct and cut-off.

## Use in the Background
Supports burnout as a relevant candidate health-impairment domain in policing while also demonstrating why operational stress, distress, trauma-related responses, and burnout should not be conflated.

## Evidentiary boundary
- Portuguese police prevalence estimates must not be transferred to Korbrimob.
- The article does not establish Digital Job Demands or POR.
- It reinforces the need to retain general operational stress as a rival explanation.

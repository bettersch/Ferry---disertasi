## Extraction
| Aspect | Content |
|---|---|
| Design/context | Survey of 405 full-time hybrid workers. |
| Constructs | Technostress; psychological detachment; burnout; work-location autonomy; workplace social support. |
| Findings | Impaired psychological detachment statistically mediated technostress–burnout associations in remote settings; location autonomy buffered a focal pathway, while workplace social support did not. |
| Relevance | Shows that digital-demand effects are context-specific and that one support construct can be null even when another resource is protective. |

## Critical appraisal
Hybrid/white-collar context differs strongly from tactical policing; survey mediation does not by itself identify causal mediation. Workplace social support is not POS.

## Statement relations
Supports the digital-demand/burnout health-impairment logic but limits any universal support-buffering claim and reinforces the need to distinguish support constructs.

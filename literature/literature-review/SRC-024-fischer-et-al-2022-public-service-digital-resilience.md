## Extraction
| Aspect | Content |
|---|---|
| Design/context | Survey of German public employees during the COVID-19 shift to telework; 380 analyzed cases from 1,189 collected responses. |
| Constructs | Digital/telework demands; individual and organizational resources; public-service resilience. |
| Findings | The public service showed resilience, and the telework shift was not as demanding as expected; resources were central to the interpretation of demands. |
| Relevance | Counterevidence to a digitalization-as-harm narrative and a public-sector example in which digital transition did not automatically produce high strain. |

## Critical appraisal
Pandemic telework differs from high-intensity tactical operations, and the resilience construct is tied to crisis-era public-service functioning rather than Ferry's exact POR formulation.

## Statement relations
LIMITS any assumption that digitalization necessarily creates harmful demands and supports explicit demand–resource configuration analysis.

## Extraction
| Aspect | Content |
|---|---|
| Design/context | Cross-sectional survey; 72 rehabilitation counselors. |
| Constructs | Perceived workplace adaptive reserve/capacity to adapt and sustain change; burnout dimensions; stress; turnover intention. |
| Findings | Greater confidence that the workplace could adapt to challenges was associated with lower burnout, stress, and turnover intention. |
| Directionality relevance | Close construct-level reverse analogue: employee-perceived organizational adaptive capacity is modeled as antecedent/correlate of lower burnout rather than burnout as predictor of adaptive capacity. |

## Critical appraisal
Small, cross-sectional, non-public-safety sample limits inference, but the construct wording is close to POR/readiness and directly reinforces the reverse-order alternative.

## Statement relations
SUPPORTS perceived organizational adaptive capacity → lower burnout association; CONTRADICTS any claim that the reverse order is empirically established as dominant.

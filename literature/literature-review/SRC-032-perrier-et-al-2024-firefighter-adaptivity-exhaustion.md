## Extraction
| Aspect | Content |
|---|---|
| Design/context | Four-wave, three-source multilevel study; 303 firefighters nested in 45 fire stations. |
| Constructs | Servant leadership; felt trust/trust climate; individual adaptivity; emotional exhaustion; collective task performance. |
| Findings | Servant leadership predicted higher adaptivity and lower emotional exhaustion through trust mechanisms. |
| Directionality relevance | Demonstrates a strong common-cause architecture in an extreme/high-reliability context: organizational leadership/trust conditions can jointly shape adaptation and exhaustion. |

## Critical appraisal
Temporal and multisource design is stronger than cross-sectional self-report evidence and context is close to Brimob's high-risk operational environment. However, adaptivity is individual-level adaptive performance, not employee-rated organizational resilience.

## Statement relations
SUPPORTS common organizational antecedents of adaptation and exhaustion; LIMITS mediation models that infer Burnout → POR without controlling leadership/trust conditions.

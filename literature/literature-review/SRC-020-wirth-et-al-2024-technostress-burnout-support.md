## Extraction
| Aspect | Content |
|---|---|
| Design/context | Cross-sectional online survey of 303 hospital nurses in Germany. |
| Constructs | Techno-overload, techno-complexity, usefulness, lack of technical support, unreliability; burnout; employer support. |
| Findings | Techno-overload and techno-complexity were positively associated with burnout; employer support moderated the lack-of-technical-support–burnout relationship. |
| Relevance | Direct evidence that specific digital demands, rather than undifferentiated technology exposure, are associated with burnout. |

## Critical appraisal
Non-police healthcare context and cross-sectional same-source design limit transfer and causal interpretation. Employer support is not identical to global POS.

## Statement relations
Supports a narrowed DJD→burnout pathway and helps distinguish digital demand facets from generic technology use; also contextualizes support as a boundary condition rather than a universal effect.

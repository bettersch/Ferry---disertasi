## Extraction
| Aspect | Content |
|---|---|
| Design/context | Cross-sectional survey of 1,279 professionals across 154 primary-care practices. |
| Constructs | Practice Adaptive Reserve (organizational capacity for change); individual response to change; burnout; hours worked. |
| Findings | Higher adaptive reserve and more positive response to change were associated with lower burnout; longer work hours were associated with higher burnout. |
| Relevance | Important alternative ordering and rival explanation: organizational adaptive capacity may shape burnout, while workload independently matters. |

## Critical appraisal
Healthcare rather than public safety; cross-sectional design prevents firm directionality despite organizational-level framing.

## Statement relations
LIMITS Burnout→POR as the only plausible ordering and SUPPORTS workload plus adaptive-capacity alternatives.

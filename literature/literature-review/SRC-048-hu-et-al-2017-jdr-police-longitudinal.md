# SRC-048 — Hu et al. (2017), longitudinal JD-R evidence in police and nurses

## Identity and provenance
- SRC ID: SRC-048
- Track: literature-review (Stage 3)
- Found by: `RUN-033`; parameter set `PRM-005`; screened with `PRM-003`
- Source type: peer-reviewed longitudinal empirical article
- Metadata: Hu, Q., Schaufeli, W. B., & Taris, T. W. (2017). How are changes in exposure to job demands and job resources related to burnout and engagement? A longitudinal study among Chinese nurses and police officers. *Stress and Health*.
- DOI: 10.1002/smi.2750
- Access: full text link returned by Elicit.

## Extraction
Two-wave data from 172 nurses and 273 police officers showed that increasing demands and decreasing/chronic-low resources were associated with increasing burnout; decreasing resources also corresponded with declining engagement. This is direct police longitudinal support for the JD-R demand/resource→well-being architecture.

## Critical appraisal
Strong context and temporal relevance for DJD/POS→Burnout logic at the generic demand/resource level. It does not test digital demands, POS specifically, or Adaptive Performance.

## Statement relations
SUPPORTS demand/resource→burnout architecture; SUPPORTS engagement as rival motivational mechanism; DOES NOT SUPPORT Burnout mediation to Adaptive Performance.

## Extraction
| Aspect | Content |
|---|---|
| Design/context | Six-month longitudinal multilevel study; 208 EMS first responders nested in 45 stations across three U.S. fire departments. |
| Constructs | Station safety climate; emotional exhaustion; safety compliance; morale; depression. |
| Findings | Higher safety climate was associated with better morale/lower depression, while emotional exhaustion was associated with poorer morale and more depression; high exhaustion attenuated the safety-climate–safety-compliance relationship. |
| Directionality relevance | Shows organizational climate and exhaustion as interacting/parallel determinants of safety and well-being over time, not evidence that exhaustion produces perceptions of organizational resilience. |

## Critical appraisal
Strong contextual fit and temporal/multilevel design. Safety climate is not POR, but the study shows a credible common-cause/parallel-outcome architecture in a high-reliability first-responder setting.

## Statement relations
CONTEXTUALIZES Burnout/POR ordering; SUPPORTS organizational-context and common-cause alternatives; LIMITS direct Burnout → POR mediation claims.

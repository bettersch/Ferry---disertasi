## Extraction
| Aspect | Content |
|---|---|
| Design/context | Multilevel survey of 10,546 U.S. Navy sailors nested in 725 departments and 101 ships. |
| Constructs | Perceived team resilience at individual/department/ship levels; job stress; physical and mental health risks. |
| Findings | Team resilience at multiple levels predicted better occupational-health outcomes; department-level resilience reduced the strength of the individual job-stress–mental-health-risk relation. |
| Directionality relevance | Strong high-reliability evidence placing collective resilience upstream of employee health and showing contextual/cross-level effects. |

## Critical appraisal
Highly relevant hierarchical high-stress context and unusually strong multilevel structure. Outcome is occupational health rather than burnout specifically, and the focal construct is team resilience rather than POR, so it supports directionality and level logic but cannot be substituted for the exact dissertation constructs.

## Statement relations
SUPPORTS top-down collective-resilience → employee-health ordering; LIMITS a one-way employee Burnout → organizational-resilience assumption.

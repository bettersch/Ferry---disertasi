## Use in Issue #11

The abstract reports employee survey data on organizational traits and organizational resilience, including employees' confidence in the organization's ability to adapt and differences in **perceived organizational resilience** across employee groups. This provides targeted evidence that employee-reported perceptions of an organization's resilience/adaptive capability can be studied as a perception-level outcome.

## What this source supports

- employee respondents can report perceptions of organizational resilience/adaptive capability;
- a person-level perception claim is conceptually different from claiming the organization itself objectively possesses a measured resilience capability;
- public-sector employee-survey precedent exists for this perceptual framing.

## What this source does not establish

- that `Perceived Organizational Resilience` is a universally standardized construct label or scale;
- that any particular instrument is valid for Korbrimob;
- that individual perceptions can be aggregated to true unit/organization-level resilience without agreement/multilevel evidence;
- that burnout causes perceived or actual organizational resilience;
- that the Ferry study should use the same model or measurement.

## Relation to existing theory

`SRC-008` (Duchek, 2020) defines true Organizational Resilience as an organizational capability/process. `SRC-010` is used only to show a defensible person-reported alternative when the empirical unit remains individual personnel. The distinction must remain explicit in naming and claims.

## GRO-01 · Introduction

- Status: VERIFIED
- Flow stage / gate: Stage 0–1 · FG0
- Version: 1.0 · 2026-10-11 · PR #2
- Basis: CTX-001; CTX-003; PHN-001; PHN-002; SRC-001; DEC-007; DEC-008
- Verified by / date: Researcher (explicit chat verification) / 11 October 2026

Korbrimob Polri is the locus of this dissertation. Its 2025–2029 strategic plan documents continued high-intensity operational responsibilities alongside technology-based operations, data analytics, and a need to adapt personnel capabilities to increasingly complex information technology. These facts establish a modernization phenomenon, not a causal effect or scientific gap. The study will therefore determine which personnel-level and organizational conditions are actually implicated by that modernization and which remain unexplained after theory and empirical evidence are reviewed. The current working title and construct set are retained only as candidates. Paradigm, theory, research questions, method, novelty, and contribution remain undecided. The immediate goal is a defensible problem framing grounded in verified institutional and scholarly evidence.

- What this forecloses: treating digitalization as proof of high Digital Job Demands, burnout, low resilience, causality, or novelty before the relevant evidence is established.
- Not yet settled: institutional requirements; output language; unit/level of analysis; final phenomenon boundary; theory; constructs; RQs; method; measurement; access/ethics; novelty; contribution.

## GRO-02 · Grand theory

- Status: VERIFIED
- Flow stage / gate: Stage 2 · FG1
- Version: 1.0 · 2026-10-11 · PR #6
- Basis: THR-001–THR-007; GAP-001; GAP-002; SRC-002–SRC-009; DEC-010–DEC-013
- Verified by / date: Researcher (explicit chat verification) / 11 October 2026

Job Demands–Resources Theory is proposed as Ferry’s primary grand theory because it directly organizes work characteristics as demands/resources and explains the health-impairment process toward burnout; its digital extensions accommodate Digital Job Demands without assuming digitization is inherently harmful. Organizational Support Theory remains a construct-specific foundation for POS, which may function as a candidate resource. Organizational resilience stays conceptually separate: Duchek’s anticipation–coping–adaptation framework defines an organizational capability, not an automatic person-level outcome. The principal unresolved theory problem is therefore the bridge from individual burnout to organizational resilience. COR remains a complementary resource-loss explanation, not a co-equal grand theory unless later evidence shows a distinct explanatory necessity.

- What this forecloses: decorative multi-theory stacking; treating POS theory or resilience theory as co-equal grand theories without distinct necessity; assuming individual burnout automatically causes organizational resilience; equating digitization with Digital Job Demands.
- Not yet settled: whether Digital Job Demands and POS survive the construct–field lock as final constructs; the exact cross-level mechanism; final Y level/label; research questions; method and measurement.

## GRO-03 · Literature and gap

- Status: VERIFIED
- Flow stage / gate: Stage 3 · FG1
- Version: 1.0 · 2026-10-11 · Issue #41 / PR #42
- Basis: LIT-001; LIT-002; LIT-010–LIT-013; GAP-003; GAP-006; SRC-048–SRC-056; DEC-019; DEC-020
- Verified by / date: Researcher (explicit continuation approval after verification request) / 11 October 2026

Empirical literature supports a demand–resource architecture rather than a burnout-mediation chain. Specific digital demands are repeatedly linked to strain/burnout, but policing evidence does not yet cleanly isolate bounded DJD from operational workload or digital-skill mismatch. POS is comparatively well supported as a lower-burnout resource and has broader evidence linking it to Adaptive Performance. Burnout shows a modest adverse performance association, but evidence is insufficient to lock it as mediator of Adaptive Performance. Digital demands may operate as challenge or hindrance demands, so their performance direction remains open. The defensible unresolved problem is how bounded DJD and POS relate to personnel health and Adaptive Performance under high-intensity policing modernization, not whether one exact four-variable model is absent.

- What this forecloses: restoring Burnout mediation for model sophistication; claiming all DJD reduce Adaptive Performance; treating absence of an exact four-variable paper as novelty; treating focused-search non-retrieval as proof of literature absence.
- Not yet settled: final RQs/hypotheses; whether DJD–Adaptive Performance is modeled as directional or exploratory; measurement choices; research design; novelty/contribution; authenticated Scopus/WoS parity.

## GRO-04 · Novelty

- Status: REPORTED
- Flow stage / gate: Stage 4 · FG1
- Version: 1.0 · 2026-10-11 · Issue #44 / PR #45
- Basis: GAP-002; GAP-003; GAP-007; LIT-014; LIT-015; NOV-001; SRC-057–SRC-063; DEC-021
- Verified by / date: —

Ferry's defensible novelty is not a new grand theory, the Korbrimob setting, or an exact four-variable combination. Existing policing research already links technology difficulty/support to stress and resources to adaptivity, while broader digital-work studies show digital demands can operate as challenge or hindrance demands. The unresolved contribution is to test, in high-intensity policing, a clearly bounded digital job-demand domain—separated from operational workload and digital-skill mismatch—against two theoretically different outcomes: Burnout and Adaptive Performance, with POS as a focal resource. This positions the study as a theory-boundary/construct-clarification and empirical-pattern test of digital JD-R, not a claim that all digital demands are harmful.

- What this forecloses: "first/only study" language; novelty from Indonesia/Korbrimob alone; novelty from merely combining four variables; universal negative DJD→Adaptive Performance; Burnout mediation as the defining contribution; claiming a new grand theory.
- Not yet settled: final RQs/hypotheses; exact DJD dimensions and measurement; challenge/hindrance operationalization; controls/rival mechanisms; design/identification; final contribution wording; authenticated Scopus/WoS parity.

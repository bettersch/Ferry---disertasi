## 1. Decision problem

After GRO-03, the remaining scholarly problem cannot be stated as "no study combines DJD, POS, Burnout and Adaptive Performance." The Stage 4 task is to determine whether there is a more substantive theoretical or empirical puzzle that justifies doctoral research.

The candidate puzzle is the **asymmetric human consequence of digital modernization**: digital-domain demands can contribute to health impairment, yet digital demands are not uniformly harmful to adaptation/performance because their content can be challenge-like or hindrance-like and because available resources shape how employees respond. In policing, this tension matters because modernization occurs while operational responsibilities remain high.

## 2. What is already known and therefore not novel

### Digital technology, support and police well-being are already studied
`SRC-057` shows direct policing research on organizational support, technology efficacy/difficulty, job satisfaction and occupational stress. Therefore Ferry cannot claim that police digital-technology demands/resources and well-being are unstudied.

### Technostress, burnout/engagement and performance combinations already exist
`SRC-058` is especially important counterevidence. In a recent anonymized public-sector Institution X under a PRESISI digital-transformation agenda, role overload and technostress were modeled with work engagement, burnout and job performance. Burnout was not a significant mediator, while engagement was a stronger performance mechanism. This sharply weakens any novelty claim based on a technostress–burnout–performance mediation configuration.

### Adaptive behavior in policing is not new
`SRC-062` and `SRC-063` show longitudinal police evidence in which resources, change communication, challenge seeking and job crafting relate to later adaptivity/engagement. Ferry therefore cannot claim that adaptation or resource→adaptivity processes are absent from policing.

### Digital Job Demands are not a new construct family
`SRC-059` and prior theory evidence already establish digital job demands/resources as an extension of JD-R. `SRC-060` further shows that distinct digital demand contents can produce divergent behavioral processes. Ferry cannot claim novelty merely by using the label DJD.

### Digitalization can already have double-edged Adaptive Performance implications
`SRC-061` links digital technology affordance to Adaptive Performance through job control while also showing a demand/threat route for other employees. Thus the broad proposition that digitalization can be both enabling and straining is not novel by itself.

## 3. Surviving substantive puzzle

A narrower problem survives these counterexamples:

1. **Construct boundary:** policing studies identified in the focused audit use technology difficulty, technostress, technology efficacy, role overload, operational stress or generic workload, while the digital-work literature differentiates digital learning demands, digital workload, digital hindrances/challenges and ICT-specific stressors. The relevant digital-demand domain for high-intensity policing is therefore not yet sufficiently bounded against ordinary operational workload and digital-skill mismatch.

2. **Asymmetric outcomes:** the health side is comparatively predictable—specific digital demands can deplete resources and contribute to burnout/exhaustion—but the performance/adaptation side is theoretically non-monotonic because digital demands can function as challenges or hindrances. A single negative DJD→Adaptive Performance hypothesis would erase this tension.

3. **Resource placement:** police evidence strongly supports organizational/supportive resources for lower strain and broader adaptation, but the strongest performance-side mechanisms also include engagement, job crafting, self-efficacy and digital literacy. POS is therefore defensible as a focal perceived resource, but not as the sole resource explanation.

4. **Field condition:** Korbrimob's documented modernization under high-intensity operations makes this tension substantively relevant, but the locus itself is not the novelty.

## 4. Defensible novelty position

**Ferry should not claim a new grand theory.** The defensible novelty is a **provisional theory-boundary + construct-clarification + empirical-pattern contribution**:

> test whether a clearly bounded digital job-demand domain—distinguished from general operational workload and digital-skill mismatch—shows **asymmetric relations with employee health impairment and Adaptive Performance** alongside POS in high-intensity policing, thereby clarifying a boundary condition of digital JD-R rather than assuming that digital demands are uniformly harmful or that burnout is the necessary performance mechanism.

This is stronger than a context-only extension because it addresses a theoretically meaningful tension: **the same digital modernization process can create resource-depleting demands while also requiring or sometimes stimulating adaptive behavior**. The contribution lies in specifying *which demand content does what to which outcome under which resource conditions*, not in the existence of four variables in one model.

## 5. Contribution class

### Theoretical contribution — bounded, not grand-theory invention
Potentially tests/refines the boundary of digital JD-R by separating health-impairment and adaptive-performance consequences and by refusing a uniform demand→negative-performance assumption. This is a theory-boundary test, not a new theory.

### Empirical contribution — primary
Examines whether the asymmetric demand/resource pattern holds in high-intensity policing where digital modernization and operational demands coexist. The focus is on the empirical pattern, not on geographical novelty.

### Construct/measurement contribution — potentially important
Requires defensible separation of relevant DJD dimensions from operational workload, technology difficulty, digital-skill mismatch and generic technostress. This contribution will only be real if measurement later demonstrates discriminant/content validity; it cannot be claimed merely from labels.

### Contextual contribution — secondary
Korbrimob provides a consequential high-intensity field setting for testing transferability and boundary conditions. Context alone is insufficient novelty.

## 6. Rival explanations that can defeat an overstated novelty claim

- general operational workload rather than digital-specific demands may account for burnout or performance;
- digital literacy/competence may determine whether technology requirements are appraised as challenge or hindrance;
- leadership, LMX, job crafting and work engagement may explain adaptation better than POS or burnout;
- staffing, objective resources, shift patterns and mission intensity may jointly cause both strain and adaptive behavior;
- common-method/cross-sectional designs could create apparent demand–burnout–performance relations without temporal identification;
- the recent Institution X/PRESISI study (`SRC-058`) already occupies part of the Indonesian public-sector digital-stress/performance space.

A doctoral contribution therefore depends on design and measurement that can separate these alternatives; novelty cannot be declared final before those decisions.

## 7. Search limitation

The novelty audit used a bounded Elicit search plus registered literature. Authenticated Scopus/Web of Science parity and exhaustive citation chasing were not completed. No "first study," "no prior study," or literature-saturation claim is justified.

## 8. Decision

**GO TO REPORT GRO-04 WITH QUALIFICATION.**

The novelty direction is sufficiently defensible to report to the researcher because it is anchored in a substantive theoretical tension and explicit construct boundary, while counterevidence has been used to narrow rather than inflate the claim.

The novelty remains **provisional until measurement/design/RQ choices preserve the distinction between digital-specific demands, operational workload, digital competence and the challenge–hindrance performance problem**.

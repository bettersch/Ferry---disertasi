# Stage 3 focused empirical synthesis — Issue #29

Status: WORKING SYNTHESIS / GRO-03 NOT VERIFIED
Date: 2026-10-11
Basis: `PRM-002`, `PRM-003`, `RUN-006`–`RUN-011`, `SRC-010`, `SRC-012`–`SRC-028`, and `literature/literature-review/matrix.md`.

## 1. What survives the empirical audit

### Digital Job Demands → Burnout
The general health-impairment logic survives, but only in a bounded form. Across digital-work studies, specific demands such as techno-overload, techno-complexity, ICT workload, hassles, poor communication, and detachment pressure are associated with exhaustion/burnout-related outcomes (`SRC-020`, `SRC-021`, `SRC-022`, `SRC-028`). Police-specific digital evidence (`SRC-012`, `SRC-013`) establishes that technology configuration can matter for officer well-being and organizational perceptions. However, the search did not identify a policing study that cleanly operationalizes the same bounded DJD construct proposed for Ferry while also separating it from general workload, technology difficulty, and digital-skill mismatch. Therefore the X1 domain remains defensible, but its policing-specific empirical support is not yet strong enough to treat the exact construct or dimensions as settled.

### POS → Burnout
This is the strongest retained relation. Police-specific evidence consistently links higher POS/organizational support with lower burnout, emotional exhaustion, or stress (`SRC-014`, `SRC-018`, `SRC-019`, with `SRC-012` as a police technology comparator). The evidence is not uniform enough to justify a universal buffering hypothesis: support source and model specification matter, and adjacent digital-work evidence includes null support moderation (`SRC-021`). The empirically safer specification is POS as a parallel organizational/job resource associated with burnout, not automatically as a DJD×POS interaction.

### Burnout as health-impairment construct
Burnout remains appropriate and contextually relevant. It has been measured directly in Indonesian Brimob personnel (`SRC-017`) and in broader police samples (`SRC-014`, `SRC-015`, `SRC-018`, `SRC-019`). The main requirement is discriminant discipline: burnout must remain separate from operational stress, general psychological distress, fatigue, trauma-related strain, and single-dimension exhaustion.

## 2. What does not survive unchanged

### Burnout → POR / mediation toward organizational resilience
The prior downstream mediation logic is not empirically established by the focused corpus. No included police/public-safety study directly demonstrates that employee burnout predicts employee-rated organizational resilience, let alone that burnout causally mediates DJD/POS effects on POR. In contrast, `SRC-023` and `SRC-027` show a plausible reverse configuration: stronger organizational resilience/adaptive reserve is associated with lower emotional exhaustion/burnout. `SRC-016` further shows that true organizational resilience in policing is shaped by organizational-level leadership, communication, governance, resources, and learning. Therefore a one-way `Burnout → POR` path is currently under-identified theoretically and empirically.

### POR as a simple outcome label
Employee-rated organizational resilience remains usable as a person-level perceptual outcome because `SRC-010` demonstrates such an approach in a public organization. Yet the focused search confirms substantial construct ambiguity. POR can overlap empirically with perceived readiness, agility, adaptive capability, or generalized organizational effectiveness. In a police/high-reliability context, construct validity must be demonstrated rather than assumed.

## 3. Rival explanations that remain serious

The literature audit strengthens rather than removes the rival set. General operational workload is independently associated with burnout-related outcomes and cannot be substituted by DJD (`SRC-015`, `SRC-027`). Digital capability/skill mismatch is related but distinct from ICT job demands (`SRC-013`, `SRC-028`). Leadership, organizational learning, governance, resource access, and staffing/operational capacity remain plausible common causes of both employee well-being and perceived/true resilience (`SRC-016`, `SRC-023`, `SRC-026`). Digital transformation can also strengthen true organizational resilience through digital capabilities and dynamic capabilities (`SRC-024`, `SRC-025`, `SRC-026`), so a one-sided 'digitalization harms resilience through burnout' narrative is not defensible.

## 4. Stage 3 judgement on the working architecture

The empirical evidence supports a **partial retain / partial revise** decision:

- **RETAIN:** bounded DJD as a candidate job-demand domain;
- **RETAIN:** POS as the strongest employee-perceived organizational resource;
- **RETAIN:** Burnout as the focal health-impairment condition;
- **RETAIN WITH CAUTION:** POR as a person-level perception of organizational adaptive/resilience capacity;
- **REVISE:** do not yet lock Burnout as the mediator carrying DJD/POS effects to POR;
- **REVISE:** do not treat the path direction between Burnout and POR as established;
- **RETAIN RIVALS:** general workload, digital capability mismatch, leadership/support source, objective resources/staffing, readiness/agility, and organizational digital capability must remain in the explanatory architecture.

## 5. GRO-03 recommendation

**REVISE — DO NOT VERIFY GRO-03 YET.**

Reason: the upstream JD-R architecture is empirically coherent, especially POS→Burnout and the broader digital-demand→strain pattern, but the downstream `Burnout → POR` and mediation bridge remains insufficiently supported and faces credible reverse-direction/common-cause evidence. A final empirical gap and final model should not be frozen until either:

1. additional targeted searching/citation chasing finds direct Burnout→POR evidence in public-safety/high-reliability settings; or
2. the model is revised so that POR is not dependent on an empirically weak mediation claim.

## 6. Immediate implication for the dissertation Background

The current Background remains defensible because it frames the four domains as an unresolved research problem rather than claiming the mediation chain is established. The closing paragraphs should continue to use cautious language such as *hubungan*, *keterkaitan*, *mekanisme yang perlu diuji*, and *persepsi terhadap kapasitas adaptif organisasi*. The Background should not state that burnout is already known to reduce Korbrimob organizational resilience.

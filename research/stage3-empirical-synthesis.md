# Stage 3 empirical synthesis — Issues #29 and #32

Status: FOCUSED SYNTHESIS COMPLETE / GAP-004 RESOLVED-REVISE-MODEL / GRO-03 NOT VERIFIED
Date: 2026-10-11
Basis: `PRM-002`, `PRM-003`, `RUN-006`–`RUN-017`, `SRC-010`, `SRC-012`–`SRC-036`, `literature/literature-review/matrix.md`, and `research/gap004-directionality-audit.md`.

## 1. What survives the empirical audit

### Digital Job Demands → Burnout
The general health-impairment logic survives, but only in a bounded form. Across digital-work studies, specific demands such as techno-overload, techno-complexity, ICT workload, hassles, poor communication, and detachment pressure are associated with exhaustion/burnout-related outcomes (`SRC-020`, `SRC-021`, `SRC-022`, `SRC-028`). Police-specific digital evidence (`SRC-012`, `SRC-013`) establishes that technology configuration can matter for officer well-being and organizational perceptions. However, the search did not identify a policing study that cleanly operationalizes the same bounded DJD construct proposed for Ferry while also separating it from general workload, technology difficulty, and digital-skill mismatch. Therefore the X1 domain remains defensible, but its policing-specific empirical support is not yet strong enough to treat the exact construct or dimensions as settled.

### POS → Burnout
This remains the strongest relation in the working architecture. Police-specific evidence consistently links higher POS/organizational support with lower burnout, emotional exhaustion, or stress (`SRC-014`, `SRC-018`, `SRC-019`, with `SRC-012` as a police technology comparator). The evidence is not uniform enough to justify a universal buffering hypothesis: support source and model specification matter, and adjacent digital-work evidence includes null support moderation (`SRC-021`). The empirically safer specification is POS as a parallel organizational/job resource associated with burnout, not automatically as a DJD×POS interaction.

### Burnout as health-impairment construct
Burnout remains appropriate and contextually relevant. It has been measured directly in Indonesian Brimob personnel (`SRC-017`) and in broader police samples (`SRC-014`, `SRC-015`, `SRC-018`, `SRC-019`). The main requirement is discriminant discipline: burnout must remain separate from operational stress, general psychological distress, fatigue, trauma-related strain, and single-dimension exhaustion.

## 2. GAP-004 directionality audit

Issue #32 specifically tested whether the weak downstream relation from the first Stage 3 reconstruction could be rescued by stronger direct, adjacent, or high-reliability evidence. Six additional searches (`RUN-012`–`RUN-017`) targeted direct `Burnout → POR`, reverse organizational adaptation/resilience → health, high-reliability analogues, and possible micro-behavior bridges.

### Direct Burnout → POR remains unsupported for model lock
The targeted corpus did not identify a qualifying study in which employee burnout credibly predicts employee-rated organizational resilience/POR. `RUN-012` and `RUN-014` returned adjacent studies but not a direct Burnout→POR test, while the exact public-safety keyword formulation in `RUN-015` returned zero displayed records. That zero is not treated as proof of literature absence because authenticated Scopus/Web of Science parity remains outstanding.

### The stronger empirical asymmetry is reverse/top-down
The evidence inspected more often places organizational or collective adaptive/resilience conditions upstream of employee health. `SRC-023` includes a longitudinal component in which organizational resilience is associated with lower emotional exhaustion. `SRC-027` and `SRC-033` associate perceived adaptive reserve with lower burnout/stress. In a large public-sector sample, `SRC-029` places perceived strategic agility upstream of engagement and well-being. Most importantly for a high-reliability context, `SRC-031` uses a multilevel U.S. Navy sample of 10,546 sailors nested in departments and ships and shows collective resilience associated with better occupational health.

### High-reliability evidence strengthens the common-cause alternative
`SRC-030` and `SRC-032` use stronger longitudinal/multilevel first-responder designs and show that safety climate, leadership, and trust can shape both adaptive/safety outcomes and emotional exhaustion. This makes a common-organizational-antecedent explanation serious: a same-source cross-sectional Burnout–POR association could arise because both are shaped by leadership, climate, support, staffing, resources, or operational conditions rather than because burnout causally produces lower POR.

### A bottom-up behavioral mechanism is plausible but is a different model
`SRC-034` and `SRC-036` show that emotional exhaustion can be associated with performance- or organizational-citizenship-related outcomes, while `SRC-035` reports a null burnout–performance relation and `SRC-030` shows exhaustion can condition safety behavior. These studies suggest that employee health impairment can matter for adaptation-relevant behavior. They do **not**, however, establish a direct or mediated Burnout→POR path. A defensible bottom-up mechanism would need explicit behavioral intermediates, temporal ordering, and preferably multilevel aggregation.

## 3. GAP-004 resolution

**`GAP-004 = RESOLVED-REVISE-MODEL`.**

Under `DEC-018`, the working architecture removes both `Burnout → POR` and `DJD/POS → Burnout → POR` as locked paths. This is not a finding that burnout is irrelevant to organizational adaptation. It is a narrower inference: the proposed mediation bridge is not sufficiently identified or empirically supported, and credible reverse/top-down and common-cause explanations make it methodologically unsound to freeze that mediation as the dissertation's core mechanism.

Burnout remains a focal employee health-impairment construct. POR may remain as a separate candidate outcome domain, but its role now depends on resolving `GAP-005` rather than on preserving the old mediation chain.

## 4. What remains unresolved

### POR as a downstream outcome
Employee-rated organizational resilience remains empirically feasible because `SRC-010` demonstrates such an approach in a public organization. Yet POR remains heterogeneous and potentially overlaps with perceived readiness, agility, adaptive capability, organizational effectiveness, and even POS. The next substantive problem is therefore `GAP-005`: whether POR is sufficiently distinct, measurable, and useful in policing to remain the downstream outcome.

### Rivals and boundary conditions
General operational workload cannot be replaced by DJD (`SRC-015`, `SRC-027`). Digital capability/skill mismatch is related but distinct from ICT job demands (`SRC-013`, `SRC-028`). Leadership, organizational learning, governance, resource access, staffing/operational capacity, trust, and safety climate remain plausible common causes of both employee well-being and adaptation-related outcomes (`SRC-016`, `SRC-023`, `SRC-030`, `SRC-032`). Digital transformation can also strengthen true organizational resilience through digital capabilities and dynamic capabilities (`SRC-024`, `SRC-025`, `SRC-026`), so a one-sided “digitalization harms resilience through burnout” narrative is not defensible.

## 5. Revised working architecture

- **RETAIN:** bounded Digital Job Demands as a candidate job-demand domain.
- **RETAIN:** Perceived Organizational Support as the strongest employee-perceived organizational resource currently supported in police evidence.
- **RETAIN:** Burnout as the focal employee health-impairment condition.
- **REMOVE FROM LOCKED MODEL:** `Burnout → POR`.
- **REMOVE FROM LOCKED MODEL:** `DJD/POS → Burnout → POR` mediation.
- **RETAIN ONLY AS CANDIDATE PENDING GAP-005:** POR as a person-level perception of organizational adaptive/resilience capacity.
- **RETAIN RIVALS:** general workload, digital capability mismatch, leadership/support source, objective resources/staffing, readiness/agility, trust/safety climate, and organizational digital capability.

No replacement downstream path is approved yet.

## 6. GRO-03 recommendation

**REVISE — DO NOT VERIFY GRO-03 YET.**

The reason has become narrower. `GAP-004` itself is no longer unresolved: the mediation path should be revised out. GRO-03 remains unverified because the downstream outcome architecture has not yet been settled. Before GRO-03 can be reconsidered, `GAP-005` must establish whether POR remains a defensible and sufficiently distinct outcome or whether a different person-level adaptation-related outcome is theoretically and empirically superior.

Authenticated Scopus/Web of Science parity and citation chasing remain required before any final literature-absence statement is used as a research-gap claim.

## 7. Immediate implications for the dissertation Background and title

The existing Background remains largely defensible because it frames the four domains as an unresolved research problem rather than claiming that burnout is already known to reduce Korbrimob organizational resilience. It should continue to use cautious language such as *hubungan*, *keterkaitan*, and *mekanisme yang perlu diuji*.

However, the current working title contains **“Peran Mediasi Burnout”**. After `DEC-018`, that phrase cannot be treated as a locked or final title claim. The title must be reconsidered after `GAP-005` and the downstream outcome architecture are resolved. A replacement title should not be finalized prematurely.

# Outcome label and level-of-analysis resolution

Status: pre-lock structural decision
Issue: #11
Basis: `SRC-001`; verified GRO-02; `SRC-008` (Duchek, 2020); `SRC-010` (Nyaupane et al., 2020); `research/construct-field-fit-matrix.md`

## Problem

The current model risks an atomistic/cross-level error if individual personnel responses are used to claim true **Organizational Resilience**. Duchek's framework defines Organizational Resilience as an organizational capability/process involving anticipation, coping and adaptation. Renstra also describes adaptation, readiness, effectiveness and governance mainly at organizational/system level.

## Decision

### Working outcome label

**Perceived Organizational Resilience (POR)** is selected as the working downstream outcome for the current person-level research architecture.

Here POR means an employee's perception of the organization's capacity to anticipate, cope with and adapt to disruption. It must not be presented as direct measurement of Korbrimob's actual organizational resilience capability.

This decision is supported by targeted public-sector precedent in `SRC-010`, where employees reported perceptions of their organization's adaptive/resilience capability. `SRC-010` does not establish a final instrument or universal scale and is used only to support the level/claim distinction.

## Intended claim level

- respondent/unit of observation: individual personnel;
- construct claim: **person-level perception of organizational resilience capability**;
- permitted wording: employee-perceived organizational resilience / perceived organizational adaptive capability;
- prohibited wording from individual-only data: “Korbrimob organizational resilience is high/low” or “burnout changes actual organizational resilience.”

## Why this is preferable to true Organizational Resilience under a person-level design

1. It removes the direct level mismatch between individual burnout and an organization-level capability outcome.
2. It remains aligned with the Renstra's organizational-adaptation domain without pretending that strategic aspirations are observed resilience scores.
3. It preserves the dissertation's interest in how personnel experience organizational adaptive capacity.
4. It allows later Stage 3 work to test whether POR is sufficiently distinct from organizational support, readiness, agility and general organizational effectiveness.

## What would be required to claim true unit/organizational resilience

True Organizational Resilience can be reopened only if the eventual data structure supports it. At minimum this would require a defensible organizational/unit-level operationalization plus evidence that person-level ratings can legitimately be aggregated or combined with unit/objective indicators. If aggregation is proposed later, within-unit agreement and between-unit variation must be demonstrated; nested units and multilevel logic would also need to be justified. This is a structural requirement, not a current method selection.

## Burnout → outcome bridge

With POR as a person-level perception outcome, the burnout→POR relation no longer contains an automatic cross-level jump. However, this only makes the levels compatible; it does **not** establish causal direction.

Plausible interpretations remain competing:
- higher burnout may be associated with more negative perceptions of organizational adaptive capacity;
- employees who perceive the organization as less resilient may experience greater strain;
- common causes such as leadership, resource scarcity, staffing or crisis severity may influence both.

Therefore the current bridge is **theoretically admissible as a same-level association for Stage 3 review, but causal mediation remains unestablished**.

## Rival outcomes retained for Stage 3 comparison

- operational readiness;
- organizational agility;
- adaptive performance/capability;
- operational effectiveness;
- service continuity.

POR should survive final lock only if Stage 3 literature shows that it is conceptually distinct and more appropriate to the research question than these alternatives.

## Issue #11 decision

- True Organizational Resilience as an individual-level survey outcome: **REJECT for the current working architecture**.
- Perceived Organizational Resilience: **NARROW/REPLACE as the working outcome label**.
- Intended level: **individual perception**.
- Burnout→POR: **level-compatible but causally unresolved**.
- Exact measurement instrument: **DEFER**.

This decision resolves the structural level problem sufficiently for Issue #12 final pre-Stage-3 construct lock.

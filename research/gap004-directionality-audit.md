# GAP-004 directionality audit — Burnout ↔ POR

Status: TARGETED RESOLUTION AUDIT  
Issue: #32  
Date: 2026-10-11  
Basis: `RUN-012`–`RUN-017`, `SRC-010`, `SRC-016`, `SRC-023`, `SRC-027`, `SRC-029`–`SRC-036`, `PRM-002`, `PRM-003`.

## Decision question
Does the empirical literature support retaining Burnout as an antecedent or mediator of Perceived Organizational Resilience (POR), or is the stronger evidence pattern reverse-direction/common-cause?

## Synthesis

### 1. Direct Burnout → POR evidence remains absent from the targeted corpus
Six targeted runs were designed to find direct and adjacent Burnout → POR/adaptation evidence. `RUN-012` and `RUN-014` did not surface a study in which burnout was a credible antecedent of employee-rated organizational resilience. The exact public-safety keyword run (`RUN-015`) returned no records. This cannot be converted into a universal absence claim because authenticated Scopus/Web of Science parity remains unavailable.

### 2. The empirical asymmetry points in the opposite direction
The strongest design evidence inspected places organizational or collective adaptive/resilience conditions upstream of employee health. `SRC-023` includes longitudinal evidence that organizational resilience is associated with lower emotional exhaustion. `SRC-031` uses a very large multilevel U.S. Navy sample and shows that team resilience at individual, department and ship levels predicts occupational health and buffers work-stress risk. `SRC-029`, `SRC-027` and `SRC-033` similarly place perceived agility/adaptive reserve upstream of better well-being/lower burnout.

### 3. High-reliability evidence strengthens a common-cause explanation
The first-responder studies `SRC-030` and `SRC-032` are especially important for Ferry because their designs are longitudinal/multilevel and their contexts involve safety-critical work. They show that organizational climate, leadership and trust can shape both adaptive/safety behavior and emotional exhaustion. This means an observed Burnout–POR association in a same-source cross-sectional survey could reflect shared organizational antecedents rather than a causal Burnout → POR process.

### 4. Burnout may still matter for adaptation-relevant behavior, but that is a different claim
`SRC-034` and `SRC-036` show that emotional exhaustion can be associated with lower performance or OCB-related pathways, while `SRC-035` provides a null burnout–performance result. `SRC-030` also shows high exhaustion can weaken safety-compliance benefits. These studies make a bottom-up mechanism plausible in principle, but they do not validate POR as its outcome. Establishing such a mechanism would require explicit intermediate behaviors (e.g., adaptive performance, knowledge sharing, safety/proactive behavior), temporal ordering and preferably multilevel aggregation.

## GAP-004 resolution

**RESOLVED-REVISE-MODEL.**

The current evidence is insufficient to retain `Burnout → POR` as a locked directional path or to retain Burnout as the mediator carrying DJD/POS effects to POR. The problem is not merely that the path is understudied; the empirical literature inspected contains a meaningful asymmetry toward reverse/top-down and common-cause explanations.

Therefore the dissertation should **remove the Burnout → POR mediation claim from the working model before GRO-03**. Burnout remains a defensible employee health-impairment construct, and POR may remain a separate candidate outcome domain, but their directional relation must be re-architected rather than assumed.

## What remains open after resolving GAP-004

1. Whether POR should remain as a separate person-level outcome after the construct-validity audit in `GAP-005`.
2. Whether a better downstream employee-level outcome is adaptive performance/readiness-related behavior rather than POR.
3. Whether a future multilevel design could test a bottom-up employee-health → collective-adaptation mechanism using explicit behavioral mediators and aggregation.
4. Scopus/Web of Science parity and citation chasing are still required before converting any search non-result into a final literature-absence claim.

## GRO-03 implication

GRO-03 remains **REVISE / NOT VERIFIED**, but for a narrower reason than before: `GAP-004` is no longer an unresolved question about whether to keep the mediation path. The path should be revised out. GRO-03 can be reconsidered only after the downstream outcome architecture—especially `GAP-005` and the role of POR—is resolved.

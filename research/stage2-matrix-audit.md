# Stage 2 Audit of Existing Ferry Literature Matrices

Status: WORKING AUDIT v0.1 — 2026-10-11  
Issue: #5  
Purpose: determine how the four pre-existing Ferry matrices may be used in the repository without silently upgrading them into independently verified systematic reviews.

## Audit rule

The matrices are valuable **working evidence maps**. Their article lists, syntheses, mixed/null findings, construct-boundary notes, and candidate gaps are inputs to Stage 2–3. Their internal quality-control `PASS` labels are not equivalent to repository quality gates G2/G3. Primary theoretical claims are rechecked against original/primary sources before entering `brain.md` as supported theory statements.

## 1. Digital Job Demands matrix

File inspected: `Digital_Job_Demands_Literature_Matrix_25.docx`.

### What the matrix contributes
- Explicitly separates Digital Job Demands from technostress/technostrain responses and from burnout.
- Maps the construct genealogy from technostress creators and ICT demands toward explicit JD-R Digital Job Demands.
- Retains mixed/null evidence: digital intensity/tool quantity does not automatically equal demand; some digital arrangements are resources.
- Identifies major threats: construct conflation, cross-sectional self-report evidence, reverse causality, and general-workload/usability/skill alternatives.
- Identifies Scholze & Hecker (2023, 2024) as the strongest direct construct/theory anchors.

### Stage 2 judgment
**RETAIN as a working construct map.** The central construct logic is independently supported by `SRC-004` and `SRC-005`. The matrix's proposed five-domain content is not yet a final measurement decision.

### Important limitation
The matrix used a hard free-full-text/recency rule that moved several seminal technology-stress sources out of the final 25. That may be acceptable for its own selection rule, but Stage 2 theory work cannot treat accessibility/recency as a reason to ignore theoretically necessary seminal sources.

## 2. Perceived Organizational Support matrix

File inspected: `Perceived_Organizational_Support_Literature_Matrix_25.docx`.

### What the matrix contributes
- Keeps POS distinct from supervisor support, coworker support, objective support, and support climate.
- Synthesizes higher POS as generally associated with lower burnout across several settings while retaining path-specific, null, suppression, and counterintuitive findings.
- Correctly treats universal buffering as unestablished and suggests that a direct resource role is better supported than automatically adding a DJD×POS interaction.
- Identifies police-specific evidence (e.g., Zeng et al.) and high-risk comparators.

### Stage 2 judgment
**RETAIN as a working resource/construct map.** Original POS identity and theory were independently checked through `SRC-006` and `SRC-007`. POS may plausibly enter JD-R as a resource, but this is not the same as saying Organizational Support Theory explains the whole dissertation model.

### Important limitation
The final 25 excluded the original Eisenberger et al. source and Rhoades & Eisenberger review because of the matrix's access rule. Stage 2 has therefore restored those sources to the theory track rather than using later substitutes as the only theoretical basis.

## 3. Burnout matrix

File inspected: `Burnout_Literature_Matrix_25.docx`.

### What the matrix contributes
- Clearly separates burnout from fatigue, depression, anxiety, technostress, and disengagement.
- Identifies JD-R as the strongest primary explanatory framework for the demand/resource→burnout process.
- Retains mixed evidence on buffering and causal direction.
- Identifies Indonesian police-specific MBI-HSS validation (Yulianto, 2020) and modern BAT alternatives, while explicitly deferring the final instrument decision.
- Flags same-source mediation and temporal-identification problems.

### Stage 2 judgment
**RETAIN as a working health-impairment map.** Burnout is theoretically compatible with the JD-R health-impairment pathway, but measurement choice and mediation specification remain later decisions.

### Important limitation
The matrix itself acknowledges that the downstream Burnout→Organizational Resilience link is the weak point and creates a level-of-analysis problem. Stage 2 does not convert that weakness into a novelty claim.

## 4. Organizational Resilience matrix

File inspected: `Organizational_Resilience_Literature_Matrix_25.docx`.

### What the matrix contributes
- Most important conceptual warning in the four matrices: Organizational Resilience is an organizational capability/process and is not interchangeable with individual resilience or an individual's perception of organizational resilience.
- Maps capability/process, realized-performance, and perceptual operationalizations as related but non-equivalent.
- Retains evidence that digitalization can increase organizational resilience while simultaneously increasing employee digital demands.
- Identifies reverse-direction/common-cause alternatives: resilient organizations may create healthier work conditions; crisis severity may raise burnout and reduce resilience simultaneously.
- Prioritizes public/high-reliability comparators and Duchek (2020) as a central conceptual anchor.

### Stage 2 judgment
**RETAIN the organizational-resilience domain, but do not lock it as the final Y construct.** `SRC-008` independently supports the organization-level capability/process definition. If eventual data are individual-only and single-level, the project must either narrow the empirical outcome to perceived/employee-rated organizational resilience or justify aggregation/multilevel/objective outcomes.

## Cross-matrix synthesis for Stage 2

The four matrices are mutually coherent on one important upstream chain:

**digital work characteristics / demands + organizational resources → employee health impairment (burnout)**

That chain is compatible with JD-R and has clear construct-separation rules. The matrices are substantially less secure on the downstream bridge:

**individual burnout → organizational resilience capability**

This is therefore not something to assume. It is the principal theoretical/level-of-analysis problem that Stage 2 must carry forward.

## Decisions the matrices do NOT justify by themselves

- final four-variable model;
- final hypotheses;
- mediation as causal mechanism;
- POS as universal moderator;
- a specific Digital Job Demands scale/dimension set;
- MBI-HSS versus BAT;
- Organizational Resilience as an individual-level score;
- mixed methods, SEM, PLS-SEM, SmartPLS, or any estimator;
- novelty or contribution.

## Repository status

The matrices have now been **inspected as working artifacts**, but a repository-grade comprehensive empirical review has not yet been reconstructed. Stage 3 must register the empirical corpus/protocol and closest-study evidence before G2/G3 can be assessed.

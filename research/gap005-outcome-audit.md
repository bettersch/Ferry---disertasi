# GAP-005 downstream outcome audit

Status: `RESOLVED-REPLACE-Y` for the working architecture
Issue: #37
Date: 2026-10-11
Primary field guideline: `SRC-001` Renstra Korbrimob Polri 2025–2029
Search basis: `PRM-004`, `RUN-018`–`RUN-028`, existing Stage 3 evidence, and `SRC-037`–`SRC-047`

## 1. Decision problem

After `DEC-018`, Burnout→POR and the old DJD/POS→Burnout→POR mediation are no longer locked. The remaining question is not simply whether POR can be measured; it is whether POR is the **best outcome** for a person-level dissertation architecture grounded in Korbrimob's documented modernization/adaptation problem.

The outcome must survive three simultaneous tests: (a) represent something the Renstra makes substantively important, (b) remain coherent with individual personnel as the candidate observation/analysis level, and (c) have a defensible nomological position relative to JD-R demands/resources and burnout without forcing an unsupported causal direction.

## 2. What the Renstra requires the outcome to represent

`SRC-001` and the field/problem map show several distinct adaptation-related targets rather than one interchangeable concept:

- adaptive and competent personnel (Renstra pp. 37–41);
- technology/data-analytics capability and training/certification (pp. 62–64);
- operational response quality, effectiveness/efficiency and capability development (pp. 74–75);
- organizational/system adaptation, governance and modernization (pp. 30, 37–41, 58–64).

Therefore the field evidence permits both organizational-capability and personnel-performance framings. It does **not** require the dissertation to retain organizational resilience merely because it appeared in the original title.

### POR

Recent evidence changes one earlier concern: organizational resilience is increasingly measurable through employee reports. `SRC-037` (BORS) validates ability/process dimensions in 1,435 employees across 138 organizations, while `SRC-038` provides an early capability-based assessment aligned with anticipation–coping–adaptation. Thus **measurement immaturity alone is no longer a sufficient reason to reject POR**.

The harder problem is structural. Police organizational-resilience evidence (`SRC-016`) emphasizes leadership, governance, communication, resources and learning, and Stage 3 directionality evidence (`LIT-006`) more often places collective/organizational adaptation upstream of employee health. Using POR as Y would therefore preserve a weak downstream ordering and same-source evaluative overlap with POS/effectiveness even if the scale itself is valid.

### Readiness

Readiness has excellent measurement maturity (`SRC-039`, `SRC-040`) and unusually strong police proximity. `SRC-044` studies perceived organizational emergency readiness in 2,733 Brazilian police officers, and `SRC-045` studies readiness for change in 622 Norwegian police employees.

However, readiness is normally **readiness for something**: a change, emergency or implementation. It also commonly depends on management support, trust, climate, communication and change efficacy. This makes readiness valuable as a comparator and possible future contextual outcome, but less clean as the general downstream consequence of DJD/POS/Burnout.

### Organizational/strategic agility

Agility maps well onto speed, flexibility, sensing and resource reconfiguration. Public-sector evidence is substantial (`SRC-029`, `SRC-041`) and police resilience research treats agility as one component/enabler of resilience (`SRC-016`). Yet this evidence normally positions perceived agility as an **organizational resource/capability upstream of employee engagement, well-being or retention**, not as an employee-health downstream outcome. Selecting agility as Y would conflict with the current direction of the strongest evidence.

### Adaptive Performance

Adaptive performance changes the substantive label rather than merely renaming POR. It is a **person-level behavioral/performance construct**: how effectively an employee adjusts behavior and performance when work requirements change. This gives it four advantages for Ferry.

1. **Level alignment.** It can be measured and interpreted at the same person level as DJD, POS and burnout without claiming an organizational capability from individual survey responses.
2. **Direct Renstra fit.** The Renstra explicitly demands adaptive/competent personnel, technology capability, performance improvement and effective IT-enabled procedures (`SRC-001`; field/problem map).
3. **Measurement maturity.** Adaptive performance has a mature research base (`SRC-042`), public-sector validation, and an Indonesian five-factor Adaptive Performance Scale validation (`SRC-043`).
4. **Cleaner nomological role.** Performance/behavior is more naturally eligible to sit downstream of demands/resources and health impairment than employee-rated organizational agility/resilience, although the exact Ferry relations are **not yet locked**.

Its main weakness is equally important: direct policing-specific adaptive-performance evidence is still limited (`RUN-028`). Replacing Y therefore solves the level/construct-placement problem but creates a narrower evidence requirement for the next audit.

### Perceived organizational effectiveness

Public-sector evidence confirms that employees can rate effectiveness (`SRC-047`), including adjacent police evidence found in `RUN-022`. But perceived effectiveness is broad enough to incorporate funding, management, participation, performance and support judgments. That breadth is a liability here because it risks becoming an omnibus evaluative outcome with high same-source overlap with POS and weak specificity to adaptation.

## 4. Decision

**`GAP-005 = RESOLVED-REPLACE-Y`.**

For the **working Stage 3 architecture**, replace POR as the focal downstream outcome with **Adaptive Performance (individual-level)**.

This is a construct replacement, not a semantic relabeling:

- POR remains a relevant contextual/comparator domain for organizational adaptation and should not be erased from the literature synthesis.
- True Organizational Resilience remains an organization-level capability and is not inferred from person-level Adaptive Performance.
- Readiness and agility remain important rival organizational/adaptation conditions.
- The original title's “Ketahanan Organisasi” and “Peran Mediasi Burnout” are therefore both under revision.

## 5. What is **not** decided

This audit does **not** approve any of the following:

- Burnout as a mediator of DJD/POS effects on Adaptive Performance;
- direct DJD→Adaptive Performance or POS→Adaptive Performance hypotheses;
- a causal ordering among the retained constructs;
- a final Adaptive Performance instrument for Korbrimob;
- a final title, research question, estimator, design, novelty or contribution.

`RUN-024` and `RUN-025` establish only that a JD-R/performance bridge is plausible and worth targeted review. They do not identify Ferry's final model.

## 6. New evidence requirement

**`GAP-006` (provisional evidence problem):** the specific empirical architecture connecting bounded Digital Job Demands, POS, Burnout and individual Adaptive Performance in policing/high-reliability work remains under-established in the focused corpus. Before GRO-03 can be reported, a relation-specific audit must test direct, indirect, reverse and rival configurations, with particular attention to general operational workload, digital capability, leadership/resources, engagement and common-method bias.

This is an evidence requirement, **not a novelty claim or proof of literature absence**.

## 7. GRO-03 checkpoint

**NO-GO for GRO-03 reporting yet.** Outcome selection is now structurally clearer, but GRO-03 should wait until:

1. the new Adaptive Performance relation architecture is audited;
2. `GAP-003` and `GAP-006` are synthesized without artificial gap claims;
3. remaining citation-chasing/database-parity limitations are stated and, where feasible, reduced.
